A **micro test** has Opens a Microbiology case enabled in the catalog. Its
**Case role** is Culture, Direct or Case. A **Program** supplies questions and a
reporting track, never routing. The **case lab unit** determines its worklist and
mutation rights; transferring it does not change its tests or identity.

## Lab Context

### Problem addressed by this design

A microbiology specimen (a sputum, a urine, a wound swab) is received once and then worked for days or weeks. At the Central Public Health Laboratory (CPHL) in Port Moresby it goes to one of three benches, each its own lab unit: the TB unit, the Microbiology unit, or the Environmental Microbiology unit. Other laboratories split the work differently, and some have one Microbiology section that does everything. The technician first looks at the specimen directly: a wet preparation, a Gram stain, an acid-fast bacilli (AFB) smear for TB, or a GeneXpert run (a molecular test that detects TB and rifampicin resistance in about two hours). Only then is it plated on growth media (inoculation) and left in the incubator. If something grows, the technician examines the colonies under the microscope, subcultures them, identifies each organism, and tests it against antibiotics: antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST) for bacteria, drug susceptibility testing (DST) for TB.

OpenELIS today models this as fixed "workflows". Each culture test declares Bacteriology or TB, and a culture setup attached to it (media, incubation hours, maximum days) decides how the case behaves. Reception fills a Microbiology box of clinical details at order entry.

### Pain

- **The workflow is declared in advance, and the bench does not work that way.** A technician decides at the bench whether a sputum needs a GeneXpert, a smear, a culture, or all three. When the declared workflow is missing or wrong, the case lands "Unassigned" and has to be reclassified. Earlier user acceptance testing found that failed reclassification could leave a case stuck.
- **Direct microscopy has no home.** A Gram stain on the specimen itself can only be recorded by creating a preliminary isolate, even when nothing has grown yet. A GeneXpert result has no place on a bacterial case at all.
- **Incubation is free text.** "48h" and "2 days" are both typed into a text box, so nothing can tell a technician that a plate is due to be read, or that a TB culture has reached its 42 days.
- **Reception is asked for things it does not know.** Prior antibiotics, TB treatment history and the reason for an environmental swab are on the request form, but typing them at the reception desk slows the queue and they are often skipped.
- **Smaller sites cannot hand over.** A district laboratory that can do microscopy but not culture has no way to send the rest of the work to CPHL and receive the results back on the same case.
- **Analyzer results can only land where a row already exists.** A GeneXpert or an identification instrument that sends a result for a test not yet on the case has nowhere to go.
- **The printed micro result is one line of text.** Isolates and susceptibilities are joined into a single remark of up to 200 characters on the patient report.

### What Changes

Reception orders micro tests (for example "Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra", "Bacterial culture" or "Blood culture", one sample per bottle) in the ordinary test picker, like any other test, and the first one opens the case. Everything else happens on the Case view. The technician fills in the case details from the request form, records the direct tests in a new **Initial testing** section (adding any test from the catalog, or a result that was already reported elsewhere), and either refers the specimen or plates it. The culture test's linked media propose the standard plating, each plate or bottle records its medium (with a lot when the medium is tracked), labels print for plates and isolates, and incubation is recorded as a number and a unit, with an optional reminder to check, so the Worklist shows "check due" and "incubation complete" without anyone counting days. Growth leads to tests on that culture picked from the catalog (a Gram stain, a ZN stain, a rapid identification), which can be kept In lab only, and to subcultures. Isolates get their default AST or DST panel automatically, and more can be added. An analyzer result with no matching row waits in one **Incoming results** panel until the technician places it. Results are entered, pass expert review (validation) and are released; the technician ticks which results go on the report, which prints inside the redesigned patient report. The case has its own Program, whose questionnaire holds the program questions, and bench sheets print from the existing Workplan.

## Overview

One case layout serves every micro lab unit, and nothing on it depends on a configured culture type. The case holds whatever tests, cultures and panels the bench adds; the breakpoints are chosen per panel; TB behaviour (resistance classification, the NTM outcome) follows the organism; and the case's **Program**, chosen in Case information, brings its questions (the TB Program's TB history) and its reporting track decides the exports.

**Case view section order (v2):**

1. Case header (lab number, patient or site, sample type, **body site and side**, **Program** and its reporting track, **case lab unit** with Change, related cases on the same sample in other lab units with a switcher, stage, priority)
2. **Incoming results** (shown only when something is waiting)
3. **Case information** (moved from order entry)
4. **Initial testing** (new: microscopy, wet prep, Gram stain, GeneXpert, molecular DST on the specimen, any added test)
5. **Referral point** (new: refer the rest of the work-up)
6. **Culture**: inoculation, incubation and the work-up of each culture (restructured). Each culture row lists under it, as a tree, the catalog tests run on it (a Gram stain, a ZN, a rapid identification) and its subcultures (A-10); there is no separate Growth work-up section
7. **Isolates** (existing)
8. **AST / DST** (existing, plus add panel or test)
9. **Additional testing** (new: tests added at the end of the work-up, for example whole genome sequencing)
10. **Critical communication** (existing; calls also go to the shared callback log, A-18)
11. **Nonconformance** (existing)
12. **Report** (existing readiness and release, plus per-result Report choice; prints inside the patient report, A-11)
13. **Amendment** (existing)
14. Timeline (existing)

Expert review is not a separate section: it is the case's validation step (A-17).

### Navigation

The Worklist opens the case at the relevant row. Case search, patient history,
shared order entry, Inventory, Test Catalog, Workplan and report views remain
linked entry points. Filters and focus survive navigation and reload.

## User Stories

1. As a **reception clerk**, I want to add micro tests in the same test picker as every other test, and nothing else, so that micro requests move through reception as fast as any other order.
2. As a **microbiology technician**, I want to add the tests I decide to do (smear, GeneXpert, Gram, a DST panel) to the case at the point I do them, so that I am not blocked by a workflow someone declared in advance.
3. As a **technician at a district laboratory**, I want to record my microscopy and refer the rest of the work to CPHL, so that the results come back to the same case and the clinician gets one report.
4. As a **technician on the culture bench**, I want to be told when plates are due to be checked and when incubation is complete, so that no culture is read late or forgotten.
5. As a **technician working at a bench with no computer**, I want to print the plates due today with each patient's name and lab number, tick them off and write the readings, and then enter them in the same order, so that nothing is missed between the bench and the screen.
6. As a **microbiology validator**, I want expert rule flags resolved before I accept results, and to choose which results appear on the patient report, so that impossible phenotypes, internal work-up and suppressed antibiotics never reach the clinician.
7. As a **laboratory manager** whose one Microbiology section does bacteriology and TB, I want a sputum’s TB culture and bacterial culture to share one case when both belong to that section, and to form related separate cases when they belong to different lab units.

## Functional requirements

### A-01. One catalog switch, Opens a Microbiology case; no culture type; culture setups are removed

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| <a id="fr-01.1"></a>FR-01.1 | **One catalog switch: Opens a Microbiology case**. The test catalog's Culture workflow attribute is replaced by a **Yes / No** switch, **Opens a Microbiology case** (default No). No means the test never opens or joins a case (serology such as RPR or Widal, a CBC, even in a micro lab unit). Yes makes it a **micro test**: culture tests (Bacterial culture, Blood culture, TB culture, Fungal culture), direct tests (Gram stain, wet preparation, AFB smear, Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra, line probe assay, screening plates) and case tests (FR-01.1b). There is no culture type: a case is not TB or bacterial by configuration. A second field, **Case role** (shown when the switch is Yes; default Direct), says what the test is on the case: **Culture** holds a culture result (a generic culture test), **Direct** is a test on the specimen, **Case** is a case test. UNASSIGNED is retired. |
| <a id="fr-01.1a"></a>FR-01.1a | **Collected in sets.** A generic culture test can be marked **Collected in sets** in the catalog (Blood culture). Its samples on one order form one case (FR-02.4a). |
| <a id="fr-01.1b"></a>FR-01.1b | **Case tests**. A micro test with Case role **Case** is a case test (for example "Microbiology case", "Wound swab for culture" or "TB case"): reception orders it on a sample to send that sample to the test's lab unit (its catalog test section) as a micro case before anyone knows which tests will be done. It holds no result of its own and prints nothing; the results added on the case print as usual (A-11), and it is complete when the case is finally released. A lab unit can have **any number of case tests**, each with its own name and sample types, so reception picks the one that reads like the request form. A new one is made by **Duplicate** on an existing case test (a copy whose name and lab unit the administrator edits) or by creating a test with the switch on and Case role Case. No other configuration is involved. |
| <a id="fr-01.2"></a>FR-01.2 | **What used to depend on the culture type now follows what is on the case**. **TB questions** (TB history, treatment month) come from the case Program's questionnaire (FR-03.7). **TB resistance classification and the NTM outcome** (A-14) apply to isolates identified as *M. tuberculosis* complex or as a non-tuberculous mycobacterium. **Breakpoints** are chosen per panel; the default follows the organism (FR-07.3). **Contaminated** is an outcome on every culture row. **Exports and surveillance** (WHONET, the antibiogram, GLASS, National TB Programme reports) choose cases by the reporting track of the case Program (FR-19.3). Sections, tests and panels never depend on a type. |
| <a id="fr-01.3"></a>FR-01.3 | **Culture setups are retired.** Remove the protocol-lane administration and case controls. Previously recorded clinical actions and their attributable history remain readable, without retaining an editable or read-only legacy configuration authority. |
| <a id="fr-01.4"></a>FR-01.4 | The culture protocol is no longer derived at order entry and is no longer shown or set on the case. Media, incubation and atmosphere are recorded per inoculation by the technician (A-05). |
| <a id="fr-01.5"></a>FR-01.5 | **AST and DST panels** are offered by organism group, with an interpretation model of clinical breakpoints or TB critical concentrations (FR-07.3), never by a case workflow type. |
| <a id="fr-01.6"></a>FR-01.6 | **Cutover preserves clinical meaning.** Existing cases keep their identities, members, results, issued reports and attributable history. Cases that would group together under the new rules remain separate and are listed for review. Cases missing a Program are assigned the clinically corresponding configured Program and listed for review with a Timeline explanation. Unresolved classifications require review; they must not fabricate clinical meaning. No retained legacy workflow or culture-setup authority remains. Engineering owns the one-way transformation. |
| <a id="fr-01.7"></a>FR-01.7 | **History stays readable.** Earlier changes to workflow, protocol and purpose remain understandable in the Timeline with their original actor, time and reason. |
| <a id="fr-01.8"></a>FR-01.8 | **Retired controls:** Change workflow, Set/change protocol, Culture setups administration, workflow-based panel filtering and Unassigned setup. No user is required to classify a workflow to work a case. |


### A-02. The case opens for the first micro test on a sample type in a lab unit; the lab unit decides who works it

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| <a id="fr-02.1"></a>FR-02.1 | **Eligible lab units** are those with at least one active micro test (FR-01.1). Nothing else marks a lab unit as microbiology. CPHL, for example, has "Bacterial culture" in Microbiology, "TB culture" in TB and an environmental culture in Environmental Microbiology; a laboratory with one Microbiology section puts all its generic culture tests there. Environmental and clinical cultures stay in separate lab units. |
| <a id="fr-02.2"></a>FR-02.2 | **Routing does not read the order's Program**. Adding a micro test does not set or lock Program, and a case opens whatever Program the order has. There is no automatic Program change, Program save guard or order-entry Microbiology section. The case has its own **Program** in Case information (FR-03.7), which defaults from the order's Program when that program is shown on micro cases. |
| <a id="fr-02.3"></a>FR-02.3 | **Reception orders micro tests like any other test, and the first one opens the case**. There is no micro section at order entry. Micro tests are picked in the ordinary test and panel picker (Clinical Order Entry v4 FR-B14) on the sample they apply to, offered for the sample types they accept, including "used as" sample types. The first micro test on a sample opens its case (FR-02.4), whether or not a culture is ordered: a sputum for Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra alone, an AFB smear alone or a Gram stain alone is a case. This is the default workflow for all microbiology work. A culture ordered later, by a reflex rule, in Edit order or with **+ Add culture** on the case, joins that case. A sample with no micro test opens no case. Saving an order does not require a physical sample: eligible requested work opens its case before collection or receipt. The responsible lab unit sees the case with **Awaiting sample**; no sample, collection time or receipt time is invented. Recording the actual sample later attaches it to the same case, and repeated saves do not create duplicate cases or samples. |
| <a id="fr-02.3a"></a>FR-02.3a | **Reception can assign a sample to a lab unit with a case test** (FR-01.1b). Ordering a case test on a sample opens a case in that test's lab unit on save, with no tests yet; the technician fills in Case information (including the Program) and adds the tests. Program on the order plays no part. Users see the case by its lab unit, as for every case (FR-12.3): a user with rights in several lab units sees the cases of all of them, and **Change lab unit** transfers it (FR-02.6). |
| <a id="fr-02.3b"></a>FR-02.3b | **Live summary before saving**. While micro tests are on the order, order entry shows a **What this order will open** panel beside the Ordered tests table (Clinical Order Entry v4 FR-B18), updated as tests and samples change, in plain sentences: one line per case it will open ("Blood culture case in Microbiology: 2 sets, 4 bottles", "TB case in TB unit: sputum -1"), the tests that stay in ordinary Results ("RPR stays in Results"), and the reflex rules that may add tests later, with the rule name ("If Blood culture result = Positive: adds Gram stain, culture"). It warns, without blocking, when a micro test will open a case in a lab unit the order has no other work in, or when a sample's tests split across lab units ("This sample opens 2 cases: TB unit and Microbiology"). Each line has a text Tag (Opens a case, Stays in Results, Referred, Tested elsewhere), never colour alone. It reads the same rules the save applies (FR-02.4, FR-02.4a, the catalog switch); it saves nothing. |
| <a id="fr-02.4"></a>FR-02.4 | **One case per order, sample type and lab unit**. The micro tests of one order on samples of the same **sample type** whose tests belong to the same **lab unit** (their catalog test section) form one case: a spot and an early-morning sputum on one order are one case; replicate environmental swabs of one type from one site are one case. For environmental samples the site is part of the grouping, because it is the case's subject (M-18 FR-D1): swabs of one type from two sites on one order open two cases. Micro tests of another lab unit on the same sample open that lab unit's case (at CPHL, the TB culture in TB and the bacterial culture in Microbiology), and the two show each other as related cases. A sample of another sample type opens its own case. A later order (a follow-up sputum next week) opens a new case, and Patient history links them (A-21). |
| <a id="fr-02.4a"></a>FR-02.4a | **Culture sets**. For a generic culture test marked Collected in sets (FR-01.1a, Blood culture), reception records **each bottle as its own sample** with its own container type, body site and collection time (Clinical Order Entry v4; for example two sets from two venipunctures are four samples: -2 aerobic and -3 anaerobic from the left arm, -4 aerobic and -5 anaerobic from the right arm). All samples of one order that carry that test form **one case**, whatever their sample type (aerobic and anaerobic bottles), site or collection time, so "coagulase-negative staphylococci in 1 of 2 sets" is read on one case and prints as one culture block. Each sample becomes its own culture row with its sample label as container identifier and its own site shown on the row (FR-05.4c). **Set number**: reception gives each bottle its set (Set 1, Set 2, ...) in the sample's row at order entry, a required field on samples carrying a test collected in sets (Clinical Order Entry v4 section N); the first bottle defaults to Set 1 and each later bottle to the set of the bottle above it. Site and collection time check the grouping but never decide it: at save, a warning (never a block) names a set with one bottle, two bottles of the same container type in one set, bottles of one set collected at different sites or more than 30 minutes apart (a lab setting), and a paediatric bottle in a set with adult bottles. **Number of sets** is the count of set numbers, shown read-only in Case information. One order is one collection episode: bottles drawn later for a new clinical episode are a new order, which opens a new case (FR-02.4). Other tests follow FR-02.4 (one case per order, sample type and lab unit); environmental samples follow the order, sample-type, lab-unit and site rule (M-18). The case header shows the set ("Blood culture: 2 sets, 4 bottles") with one line per set ("Set 1: left arm, 07:25, aerobic and anaerobic | Set 2: right arm, 07:31, aerobic and anaerobic"), and a warning Tag on a set that drew a set warning and the header overflow menu offers **Split into separate cases** (a member sample with no results on the case moves to its own related case; reason required; recorded on both Timelines). |
| <a id="fr-02.5"></a>FR-02.5 | **Which tests join a case.** A case holds the micro tests on its samples that belong to its lab unit when they are ordered. Tests with the switch off (a CBC on the same tube, a chemistry on the same fluid, serology in the micro lab unit) are not part of the case and stay on the ordinary Results and Validation screens. Changing the case's lab unit (FR-02.6) never moves tests in or out. When a test could join either of two related cases, it joins the one opened first, and only a member sample with no results may be split into its own related case with a reason on both Timelines. Regrouping existing results and joining cases are deferred. |
| <a id="fr-02.6"></a>FR-02.6 | **Change lab unit** transfers the case to the destination Worklist and edit rights. Record who, when, from and to on the Timeline; no reason is required. The case keeps its identity, Program, member samples and tests. Offer only eligible lab units in its domain, and require Results rights in both units. If another case already has the same order, sample type and destination unit, **both remain separate**. Joining existing cases is deferred. Renaming, splitting or merging lab units never merges cases. A deactivated unit leaves its cases searchable with a prompt to transfer. Transfer after final release requires an amendment (A-17). |
| <a id="fr-02.7"></a>FR-02.7 | **Change culture type is retired**: a case has no type. What is done changes by adding or cancelling tests; how the case is reported changes by changing its Program (FR-03.7). |
| <a id="fr-02.8"></a>FR-02.8 | **Edit order** (inside the step's all-or-nothing save). Adding a micro test after save opens the case or joins it. Cancelling the last micro test on a case with no results cancels the case after a confirmation (No Hard Delete); with results, it needs a reason and the case keeps its results as cancelled. Cancelling every test on the sample does the same. |
| <a id="fr-02.9"></a>FR-02.9 | **Electronic orders** arriving with a micro test open the case when the order is accepted, like a manual order. |
| <a id="fr-02.10"></a>FR-02.10 | **Reflex across cases.** A reflex rule that adds a micro test of another lab unit opens that lab unit's related case on the same sample; one that adds a culture to an Xpert-only case (rifampicin resistance detected) adds it to that case. A reflex that adds an ordinary test places it in the case whose result triggered it. |
| <a id="fr-02.11"></a>FR-02.11 | **A Tested elsewhere test** (Clinical Order Entry v4 FR-B20; no sample) is not part of a case. To bring such a result into a case, the technician records the result as Tested elsewhere on the case (FR-06.3). A received isolate is the exception (FR-02.13). |
| <a id="fr-02.12"></a>FR-02.12 | **UAT P-1 and P-2** (the duplicate "Microbiology" program and the Bacteriology program wired to the wrong test section) no longer affect routing, since routing never reads Program; the duplicates are cleaned up in the migration as data hygiene. |
| <a id="fr-02.13"></a>FR-02.13 | **Received isolates**. An isolate sent by another laboratory for identification, AST or referral is ordered like any sample: reception picks the **Isolate** sample type (FR-10.1c) and the micro tests to do here (for example the generic culture test and an AST panel), and ticks **Tested elsewhere** on the generic culture test (Clinical Order Entry v4 FR-B20). Tested elsewhere asks for the **sending laboratory** (required, the Organizations list picker, defaulting to the referring facility; no free text) and offers the **reported organism** (optional, the organism list), which is the culture result the sender reported. Unlike FR-02.11, this Tested elsewhere test has a sample, so it opens the case (one case per order, sample type and lab unit, FR-02.4). The case opens with **ISO-1** already present, tagged **Received from {laboratory}**, and its culture side closed as **Growth (received isolate)**: there is no inoculation of a primary specimen, and a purity or identification subculture is added as on any isolate (A-10). Case information adds **Original specimen type** (required before final report, FR-03.4; the sample types list, no free text), which surveillance uses (FR-19.5a); the collection date is the sample's collection date. The sender's reported organism stays on ISO-1 as **Reported identification**, separate from the identification made here; when they differ, validation shows "Identification differs from the sender's" for the validator to acknowledge (A-17). |

| ID | Requirement |

### A-04. Case header shows what the case is and who works it

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| <a id="fr-04.1"></a>FR-04.1 | The header shows the **Program** and its reporting track (or "Program not set"), the **case lab unit** (with Change lab unit), sample type, **body site and side** (for a case with several samples, "4 samples, 2 sites" linking to the Samples list, FR-04.3), the stage, and a **Related cases** switcher covering cases that share a sample and cases linked by a split. Each link identifies the case, its samples and its current lab unit. Links remain available within the same lab unit and after transfers; split cases remain related even when they no longer share a sample. Merely sharing an order does not make all its cases related. #### Susceptibility history and review

Record method and original measurement separately from source: MIC in µg/mL or
zone diameter in mm, plus the selected breakpoint standard/version and matched
level. Changing the active standard or published panel never changes old results.
Retain panel provenance, instrument/software version, instrument interpretation,
expert flags, completion time and message codes. A mismatched organism,
interpretation, missing breakpoint, expert flag or QC failure needs review before
validation; receipt alone is not validation. Show unresolved rows and explain why.

Overrides and reverts require a validator and reason, retain the original and
show the reading history. QC failure supports invalidation and a new attempt or
a justified authorized override. Repeat/retest may cover a whole panel or one
agent, with reason, method and the original attempt retained. Lot eligibility
and requiredness follow the reagent link and method. Panel adjustment records its
reason and preserves the published version used by each attempt. Per-agent
report selection follows FR-11.5. Missing values cannot silently pass review.

#### Identification history and specimen disposition

An isolate can begin with preliminary identification and then receive final
identification, method, date, significance and its source culture. Identification
history retains each previous organism and author. Reidentification requires a
validator and reason; after final release it also requires an amendment. It
never silently reinterprets historical susceptibility readings. Preserve the
sender's reported identification separately from the identification made here.
An unassigned organism is clearly pending; susceptibility setup requires an
identified organism. Default significance may be overridden with attribution.

The case header retains last activity, related cases, progress/next-step guidance,
nonconformance counts and amendment banners. Report nonconformance uses the
shared workflow. Mark specimen lost records the reason and rejects the affected
tests without discarding their work/history; repeat/retest dispositions link back
to the source work. Cancellation and rejection are attributable terminal actions.

### A-17. Case stages, validation and release

The built stage list is linear and ends at the first release. v2 separates **where the work is** from **what has been released**, so a culture keeps progressing after a partial report.

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| <a id="fr-17.1"></a>FR-17.1 | **Work stage** is the furthest point any open part of the case has reached (each inoculation row, subculture and isolate counts separately, FR-05.4c): **Received**, **Initial testing**, **Incubating** (an inoculation is open), **Growth detected** (a row has growth), **Identification**, **AST / DST in progress**, **Results entered** (no open work; results await validation), **Validated**. It moves back when late work reopens it (FR-05.7) or a new test is added. |
| <a id="fr-17.2"></a>FR-17.2 | **Culture outcome** closes the culture side: **Growth** (with isolates), **No growth** (every row No growth), **Contaminated** (every row contaminated and no repeat pending), **NTM identified** (an isolate identified as a non-tuberculous mycobacterium, FR-14.4), or **No culture ordered** / **No culture performed** (FR-17.3). |
| <a id="fr-17.3"></a>FR-17.3 | **No culture ordered or performed.** A case with no culture test (Xpert only, smear only, a negative screening plate) closes its culture side automatically as **No culture ordered**: nothing about culture prints, and the case is validated and released on its direct results. | <a id="fr-17.4"></a>FR-17.4 | **Referred** is shown beside the work stage while any referral is open (FR-08.2); it does not replace it. |
| <a id="fr-17.5"></a>FR-17.5 | **Validation (expert review).** Results are **Awaiting validation** when recorded and **Validated** when a validator accepts them (or **Returned** with a reason), the OpenELIS analysis statuses used elsewhere. On a case, validation is where expert review happens: expert rule flags acknowledged with a reason, overrides reasoned, QC, organism mismatch and no-breakpoint warnings resolved, and discordant TB drugs resolved (FR-14.3). An AST or DST run is validated with the built Review run / Accept results; Initial testing, culture and Additional testing results are validated per result. A lab can block self-validation: with the new site setting **Block self-validation** on, the user who entered a result cannot validate it, here or on the Validation page, and the action says why ("You entered this result; another validator must validate it"). | <a id="fr-17.6"></a>FR-17.6 | **Releases** are separate from the work stage and use the report states every OpenELIS report has: **Partial**, **Final** and **Amended**; there are no micro-only release types. **Release partial report** can be used as often as needed before final (for example after the Gram stain, AFB smear or Xpert, and again after identification with AST/DST to follow) and needs at least one validated, ticked result; what is still pending prints "To follow" or "In progress", so the content shows how far the work has got. **Final** needs: a culture outcome (FR-17.2), every ticked result validated, the "required before final report" fields (FR-03.4), every referral for referred culture or AST/DST work returned or cancelled, and the validation, QC, identification and required-result checks above. | <a id="fr-17.7"></a>FR-17.7 | **After final release nothing changes without an amendment.** Case information, results, readings, placements and moves, Report choices, In lab only, lab unit and Program are read-only; arriving results wait as Needs amendment (FR-09.4b). | <a id="fr-17.8"></a>FR-17.8 | **Stage transitions** replace the built linear table with the rules above: any work stage can move to the next when its condition is met; Growth detected and later stages can move back when work reopens; releases are allowed from any work stage that meets FR-17.6; Rejected and Cancelled are terminal (No Hard Delete). |


#### Issued history and readiness

Opening, cancelling and releasing an amendment each records a reason and actor.
An amended report identifies the version it corrects; the issued original remains
immutable and readable. A released no-growth report remains available when late
growth reopens work through an amendment. No-growth recording alone never
publishes a final report. Readiness names the missing outcome rather than an
isolate when no isolate is applicable; isolate-only checks show Not applicable.
Clinical-release readiness reflects the release action actually available.
Export readiness is separate, clearly labelled, and never implied by final release.

## Later (not in v2)

Agreed direction for work after v2, so it is not lost. Nothing here is included in V2 delivery acceptance.

- Joining existing cases, including at lab-unit transfer, is deferred. Automatic grouping, adding tests and the no-result split remain in V2.
- [Antibiogram](m-13-antibiogram.md), [GLASS](m-15-glass-fhir-surveillance.md) and [cluster detection](m-16-cluster-detection.md) are future capabilities; their population boundaries inform V2 exports but their delivery is not claimed.

- **Configurable final-release rules**. Follow the rule of the rest of OpenELIS: every reportable test on the case must be validated or cancelled before Final, so a laboratory that wants solid culture or PZA finished first simply has them on the case. A validator can release Final past named pending tests with a reason; they print "To follow" and arrive by amendment. No rule builder and no new setting. This would replace the v2 exemption for Additional tests (FR-15.7).
- **Six Worklist areas** (Mohamed 2.1): Plate setup, Culture monitoring, Plate reading, Identification, AST, Final review, each with critical, overdue and due-soon counts. Most already exist as the Cultures and AST views, their tiles and chips (draft 10.4 adds the overdue time and STAT-first sorting). Whether labs share any ranking of Needs attention reasons is part of this question.
- **CSV export of Program questionnaire answers.** FHIR already carries them. No CSV or report reads them today, so questions moved into a questionnaire (TB history, recent antibiotics) reach no CSV. One option: "Include Program questions" in the micro export, one column per question link ID.
- **Blood culture fill volume** (Mohamed 3.2), entered or from the instrument, with under- and overfill flags by local policy.
- **Release rights under the flexible RBAC.** Partial release with Results rights, final and amendments with Validation (kept for now); the new RBAC must let each be assigned separately.
- **Patient privacy on shared screens**. One site setting, "Hide patient names on worklists" (default off), for every OpenELIS worklist, not only micro: rows show the lab number, sample type, and age and sex, and the name shows after opening the case or result. Printed Workplan sheets keep names.
- **Case regrouping after results**. Before final release, a user with Validation rights in the case lab unit (a separately assignable permission under the flexible RBAC) could Split a member sample that has results into its own related case, or Merge two cases of the same order, sample type and lab unit. Each needs a reason and first shows an impact preview (results, readings, isolates, AST and DST runs, referrals, critical calls and report versions that move); every result stays linked to its sample; both Timelines record it. Cases on different orders are not merged but Linked as related. After final, only through an amendment. In v2, Split stays limited to a sample with no results (FR-02.4a).

## Out of scope

- Micro-specific colony count or CFU/mL fields: quantitative cultures are catalog setup (FR-05.4e).
- Changes to breakpoint content (M-02).
- A lab-unit "type" or micro marker (explicitly rejected,; generic culture tests do this job).
- Automatic placement of incoming results without a person accepting them.
- **Micro QC** (media and stain QC by lot, ATCC control strains against EUCAST QC ranges, MGIT positive and negative controls, EQA scoring). Belongs with M-12 reagent linkage and the manual QC work; flagged as a gap from the CPHL breakdown.
- **Isolate storage** (-80 °C with freezer location). Candidate for the existing sample storage feature applied to isolates; flagged as a gap.
- Structured micro results in electronic result delivery (FHIR DiagnosticReport); M-15 territory. Until then the REMARK projection carries the summary (FR-11.6).
